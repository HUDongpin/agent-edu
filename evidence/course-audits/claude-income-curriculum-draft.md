## Course promise and truth contract

This course teaches learners to build useful Claude-assisted offers, validate demand, deliver with evidence, control costs, and earn trust. It does not teach prompt resale, passive-income shortcuts, speculative earnings arithmetic, deceptive automation, or mass unsolicited outreach.

> This course teaches practical ways to use Claude in services, workflows, software, and product experiments. It does not promise income or business success. Results depend on your expertise, market demand, sales, execution, quality control, costs, and compliance with applicable terms and law. Verify Claude's outputs before using or selling them.

Practitioner examples are attributed self-reports, not representative outcome evidence. Revenue screenshots, follower reactions, repository stars, commit counts, demonstrations, payment buttons, and projected totals do not establish profit, demand, correctness, security, or causation.

The learner remains responsible for customer commitments, permissions, privacy, intellectual property, billing, taxes, professional obligations, verification, and compliance. This course is not legal, financial, investment, medical, or other regulated professional advice.

## Prerequisites and learning outcomes

Learners need basic Claude familiarity, one domain skill they can verify, and access to potential users for ethical customer discovery. A paid Claude plan is not required for every lesson.

By the end of the course, a learner can:

1. choose among a service, managed workflow, and software path without treating Claude itself as the offer;
2. distinguish attention, problem evidence, sample review, and paid commitment;
3. scope and price a pilot using observable acceptance checks and full delivery costs;
4. convert a commercial scope into a versioned delivery specification;
5. isolate client context with an approved input manifest and least-privilege folder boundary;
6. produce a decision brief with a verified claim-to-source matrix and uncertainty register;
7. hand off editable files whose calculations, sources, rendering, and limitations survive review;
8. package stable know-how as a tested Skill and map Connector permissions;
9. use an Artifact to test one buyer decision without confusing publication with a business;
10. supervise a bounded Claude Code change and measure API cost per accepted outcome;
11. sell with inspectible proof, permission-based outreach, and recurring-value measures; and
12. run a seven-day demand experiment and make a documented go, revise, or stop decision.

## Unit 1: Prove value before you build — 195 minutes

Choose a buyer, verify an expensive problem, and turn it into a bounded offer. The unit outcome is a priced pilot hypothesis backed by customer evidence and explicit stop rules.

### Lesson 1: Choose a money path that starts with expertise — 65 minutes

**Objective:** Choose one buyer, one painful job, one outcome, and one Claude-assisted path without treating Claude itself as the offer.

**Core instruction:** Buyers pay for a useful, inspectible decision, artifact, workflow, or result, not prompt volume. Learners start with a knowledge wedge they can independently review, then compare a service, managed workflow, and software. A service usually reaches buyer evidence first; software follows repeated demand and stable acceptance criteria. Repositories and posts are hypothesis sources, not forecasts. The lesson ends with a falsifiable thesis naming buyer, trigger, workaround, deliverable, reviewer, and stop evidence.

**Workflow:** list three tasks you can judge; name buyer and urgent trigger; choose a path from repeatability and self-service readiness; write the falsifiable thesis; define continue, revise, and stop evidence.

**Economics:** Contribution per delivery = collected fee minus Claude/tool cost, contractor cost, acquisition cost, refund allowance, and operator time.

**Practice:** Create a one-page money-path memo (30 practice minutes): a comparison table, falsifiable thesis, and stop rule. The buyer and outcome must be clear, the experiment must not require a full product, and no confidential or unsupported high-risk work may enter it.

**Quality boundary:** reject prompt-access offers, spam-dependent plans, screenshot-derived income claims, and unsafeguarded high-impact decisions.

**Real UI:** `fig-01-chat-composer`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `academy-work`, `commercial-terms`, `github-marketingskills`, `github-seo-audit`, `github-open-saas`, `github-shotgun-case`.

### Lesson 2: Validate paid demand before automating — 60 minutes

**Objective:** Run a demand test that separates curiosity, workflow pain, willingness to review, and willingness to pay.

**Core instruction:** Evidence strengthens as a buyer contributes something scarce: time, a recent example, sanitized input, sample review, written pilot scope, or payment. Interviews examine the last real occurrence instead of hypothetical enthusiasm. Claude may structure de-identified notes but may not invent or backfill quotes. A bounded manual sample tests the buying decision; unlimited free customization is not demand.

**Workflow:** recruit five people with recent experience; ask about the last case; code facts, quotes, interpretations, and unknowns; create one limited sample from synthetic or approved data; request a concrete review, introduction, pilot-scoping, or decline action.

**Economics:** expected pilot value remains a hypothesis until observed problem cost can support delivery and switching cost.

**Practice:** Run five consented interviews (90 practice minutes), create a de-identified evidence matrix, test a limited sample with at least two participants, and write a continue/revise/stop memo. Completion requires three recent consequential cases and two recorded sample-review actions, including declines.

**Quality boundary:** preserve consent and the full evidence trail, minimize personal data, and measure behavior rather than satisfaction.

**Real UI:** none.<br>
**Source IDs:** `academy-work`, `help-artifacts`, `github-shotgun-case`, `github-seo-audit`, `aup`.

### Lesson 3: Scope and price an offer you can deliver — 70 minutes

**Objective:** Define inputs, outputs, review, exclusions, change control, and a price hypothesis without inventing value claims.

**Core instruction:** A professional scope names the supported decision, client inputs, output format, evidence standard, reviewer, revision rounds, timing, and exclusions. Promise a verifiable process and artifact, not revenue, ranking, funding, legal approval, or another uncontrolled outcome. Estimate low, likely, and high delivery cost. A written change note covers altered inputs, volume, stakeholders, outputs, timing, risk, and approval. Client agreements separately address data, files, reusable methods, confidentiality, and third-party rights.

**Workflow:** convert one demand signal into one pilot decision and deliverable; list inputs and owners; write independently checkable criteria; estimate three cost scenarios; set fee, revision boundary, payment milestone, and change control.

**Economics:** Price floor = acquisition + setup + model/tool cost + review labor + delivery labor + revision allowance + overhead + risk allowance.

**Practice:** Write a two-page paid-pilot scope (50 practice minutes) with five pass/fail checks, two exclusions, three cost scenarios, explicit assumptions, and a peer-reviewed version 1.1 change log.

**Quality boundary:** reject unlimited revisions, undefined data volume, output guarantees, and pricing that hides review, acquisition, refunds, or connected-tool costs. Templates are not legal advice.

**Real UI:** none.<br>
**Source IDs:** `commercial-terms`, `docs-cost`, `github-seo-audit`.

## Unit 2: Deliver work a client can inspect — 205 minutes

Use Claude in a controlled service workflow with specifications, isolated context, citations, and human review. The unit outcome is a repeatable delivery packet with source evidence, acceptance checks, and a client-safe handoff.

### Lesson 4: Write a delivery spec, not a magic prompt — 60 minutes

**Objective:** Create a prompt package another qualified operator can run and review against the same acceptance criteria.

**Core instruction:** The prompt becomes an execution specification containing audience, decision, authorized inputs, definitions, constraints, evidence rules, output schema, refusal behavior, and checks. Separate stable workflow instructions, client-specific inputs, and independent review. Missing or conflicting evidence remains `NOT FOUND` or unresolved. Test normal and adversarial fixtures; the generating response cannot be the sole judge of its work.

**Workflow:** copy acceptance criteria from the pilot; separate stable and case layers; define schema and not-found behavior; run normal and adversarial fixtures; independently verify and log corrections.

**Economics:** reuse creates margin only when setup and correction time fall without lowering acceptance quality; track first-pass and verified-delivery time separately.

**Practice:** Build a delivery-spec pack (45 practice minutes): stable prompt version 1.1, case-input form, two fixtures, and review checklist. The adversarial run must preserve unsupported fields and a second reviewer must reproduce the decision.

**Quality boundary:** keep client secrets out of reusable instructions and require source pointers, discrepancy logs, refusal behavior, and human approval.

**Real UI:** `fig-01-chat-composer`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `docs-prompting`, `academy-work`, `commercial-terms`.

### Lesson 5: Run client work in isolated Projects — 70 minutes

**Objective:** Set up a client workspace with an approved input manifest, least-privilege folder boundary, and clean handoff and deletion plan.

**Core instruction:** Each Project serves one client outcome and contains only approved knowledge. Cowork sessions run in Anthropic's cloud, and local files that Cowork accesses are processed there. Use a dedicated engagement folder with approved copies and a manifest, never a broad root, multi-client archive, or sole copy. Approval behavior varies by mode and tool: Skip all approvals does not check actions, computer use lacks ordinary per-action tool checks, and permanent deletion still asks for explicit permission. Destructive, external, financial, account, and publishing actions retain human review. Ownership, export, retention, and deletion are designed before upload.

**Workflow:** create an outcome-named Project; create a dedicated folder and manifest; add minimum knowledge; set approval behavior from risk and test synthetically; export editable outputs, limitations, source map, and retention record.

**Economics:** include isolation setup and closeout because they reduce expected rework, confidentiality, and handoff costs.

**Practice:** Build a fictional Project pack (45 practice minutes) using three synthetic files. Deliver the folder, manifest, operating note, editable handoff, and retention log. A reviewer must reconstruct what Claude accessed and what the provider approved.

**Quality boundary:** never mix clients, grant broad folder access for convenience, upload unlisted inputs, retain credentials, or reuse client content without permission.

**Real UI:** `fig-02-cowork-composer`, `fig-04-new-project`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `help-projects`, `academy-cowork`, `help-cowork-safety`, `commercial-terms`, `aup`.

### Lesson 6: Deliver citation-grade research briefs — 75 minutes

**Objective:** Create a decision brief with a claim-to-source matrix, uncertainty register, and human verification record.

**Core instruction:** The product is the decision packet, not search-session length. Research may accelerate discovery, but every material claim passes four checks: identity, source content, fit to exact wording, and currency. Claims are labeled fact, practitioner report, inference, or hypothetical. Deliver the answer, source matrix, method, contradictions, unanswered questions, volatility, and next verification step. Higher-risk conclusions require qualified review.

**Workflow:** define buyer decision and source rules; use Research for discovery and preserve the trail; open every material source; classify claims and unresolved evidence; deliver the brief, matrix, method, and verification log.

**Economics:** narrow the decision and price verification time; a citation-bearing draft is intermediate work, not the finished product.

**Practice:** Create a five-claim brief (60 practice minutes) for a non-regulated decision. Deliver a one-page answer, verified matrix, and method/uncertainty note. Another reviewer must reproduce each claim.

**Quality boundary:** do not accept snippets, aggregators, inaccessible pages, unchecked quotations, or anecdotes as verified general results.

**Real UI:** `fig-03-tools-menu`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `help-research`, `commercial-terms`, `aup`, `academy-work`.

## Unit 3: Turn delivery into an operating system — 215 minutes

Create editable files, reusable Skills, least-privilege connections, and testable prototypes without hiding the operator. The unit outcome is a versioned workflow another qualified person can run, verify, and roll back.

### Lesson 7: Deliver files that survive review — 70 minutes

**Objective:** Produce a client handoff pack that remains useful after Claude and the provider leave the room.

**Core instruction:** The buyer's next action determines the editable master. Spreadsheets expose formulas and assumptions, documents retain structure, presentations retain narrative and charts, and PDFs provide a fixed view but not the only editable handoff. Acceptance happens in the actual file and target application. Open, recalculate, render, and inspect the work, then supply a source manifest, change log, limitations, and operating note. Record network and package access.

**Workflow:** identify next action; choose editable master and schema; assemble from approved inputs; open, recalculate, render, and inspect; package master, export, manifest, change log, limitations, and operating note.

**Economics:** price generation, application-level inspection, correction, and handoff documentation.

**Practice:** Build and audit one file pack from synthetic data (55 practice minutes). Deliver an editable master, optional export, ten-check QA record, and manifest. A second person must edit it and find every assumption/source; rendered output must have no blank, clipped, broken, or inaccessible content.

**Quality boundary:** reject screenshot and flattened-only handoffs, unexplained numbers, preview-only review, hidden metadata, and unapproved network access.

**Real UI:** `fig-05-file-outputs`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `academy-work`, `academy-cowork`, `commercial-terms`, `aup`.

### Lesson 8: Standardize with Skills and least-privilege Connectors — 75 minutes

**Objective:** Create a reusable operating package with an owner, license review, fixture test, permission map, and rollback path.

**Core instruction:** A Skill packages reviewed definitions, process, schema, examples, error handling, checks, and domain judgment. Current official help lists Skills for Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise, but use requires Code execution and file creation to be enabled; Team and Enterprise access can also depend on organization and administrator settings. Every Skill has an owner and exact version. Borrowed components require license evidence at the actual directory and pinned revision. Practitioner libraries show packaging, not demand or income. Map every Connector's reads, writes, external effects, credential ownership, revocation, logs, and consent. Begin read-only with synthetic records and no external action.

**Workflow:** collect stable instructions/corrections from three deliveries; create a versioned Skill with fixtures and rollback; verify licenses; map Connector permissions; test synthetically and run a supervised pilot.

**Economics:** recurring cost includes Skill maintenance, regression tests, Connector administration, and incident response; standardization helps only when setup and correction effort fall.

**Practice:** Package a Skill spec (60 practice minutes) with four synthetic fixtures, discrepancy log, and optional read-only permission map. A second operator must reproduce the decision without client data or an external action.

**Quality boundary:** no production connector, credential, client secret, unlicensed example, broad write access, hidden external action, or untested update.

**Real UI:** `fig-07-skills-settings`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `help-skills`, `help-connectors`, `github-anthropic-skills`, `github-marketingskills`, `github-claude-marketing`, `aup`.

### Lesson 9: Prototype the buyer's decision with Artifacts — 70 minutes

**Objective:** Use an Artifact to learn whether a real user can complete one valuable task and define evidence required before further investment.

**Core instruction:** Prototype one user, input, transformation, output, and decision. Ask discovery questions before interface generation. Current official help says Code execution and file creation must be enabled for Artifacts to work. An Artifact can make the workflow reviewable, but publication is only a test surface. Five target users attempt one task without coaching; record starts, comprehension, completion, interpretation, next action, errors, and workarounds. Apply a prewritten advance/revise/stop threshold.

**Workflow:** write task and decision; ask three discovery questions; build one synthetic input-to-decision path; observe five uncoached users; apply the threshold.

**Economics:** prototype return = value of a faster evidence decision minus build/test cost. Hypothetical future revenue is excluded.

**Practice:** Run a five-user test (75 practice minutes). Deliver the Artifact, observation log, and decision memo. Users must attempt without creator coaching and the decision must use behavior and commitment, not visual preference.

**Quality boundary:** do not publish client, personal, payment, credential, secret, or unlicensed material. A URL is not evidence of distribution, payment, demand, security, reliability, or support.

**Real UI:** `fig-06-artifact-workspace`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `help-artifacts`, `commercial-terms`, `aup`, `github-shotgun-case`, `github-open-saas`.

## Unit 4: Build, sell, and retain responsibly — 280 minutes

Move from prototype to software only when evidence supports it, then sell with truthful proof and accountable operations. The unit outcome is a seven-day demand experiment and a go, revise, or stop decision grounded in evidence.

### Lesson 10: Build software only after the workflow earns it — 95 minutes

**Objective:** Plan and verify one narrow software slice with tests, security boundaries, observability, and a current-price unit-economics worksheet.

**Core instruction:** Claude Code begins by documenting the validated task, inspecting the repository and architecture, locating patterns, and proposing the smallest tested change. Use a plan file, bounded branch/worktree, small diffs, explicit tests, and durable handoff. Auto mode changes the permission question but not accountability. Billing, authentication, authorization, migrations, destructive operations, secrets, and deployment remain high risk. Unit economics use accepted outcomes and include tokens, retries, tools, retrieval, storage, observability, moderation, failures, and human escalation. A release also needs limits, logs, rollback, a kill switch, and an incident owner.

**Workflow:** map validated behavior to one slice; inspect and plan; implement in a bounded branch with tests/review; calculate dated economics from measured runs and current prices; release only behind controls.

**Economics:** Cost per accepted outcome = model + tool + retrieval + retry + observability + support + human-escalation cost, divided by accepted outcomes.

**Practice:** Specify and cost one software slice (90 practice minutes). Deliver the task contract, test/review record, at least ten synthetic run measurements, dated low/likely/high economics, and release/rollback checklist. Implementation is optional; reproducible acceptance evidence is required.

**Quality boundary:** non-production and synthetic data only. Do not use credentials, charge payments, contact users, deploy, or run destructive migrations. A demo is not production evidence.

**Real UI:** none.<br>
**Source IDs:** `academy-code-101`, `blog-auto-mode`, `docs-cost`, `commercial-terms`, `aup`, `github-open-saas`, `github-lastsaas`, `x-elvissun`.

### Lesson 11: Earn trust, proof, and recurring work — 65 minutes

**Objective:** Create a truthful proof pack and retention review that distinguishes revenue, profit, time saved, quality, and buyer outcomes.

**Core instruction:** The proof ladder moves from inspectible artifact, to reproducible process, to client-approved measured outcome only when evidence permits. Repositories, creative outputs, and posts remain artifact or self-report evidence. Outreach uses warm introductions, allowed communities, partners, permission-based research, or small fit-qualified requests. A retainer requires a recurring trigger, decision, input owner, calendar, service level, acceptance measure, and stop/redesign review. Reports separate volume, accepted outputs, corrections, turnaround, cost, buyer use, incidents, revenue, and profit.

**Workflow:** expose a synthetic sample and checks; create a claim ledger; ask fit-qualified buyers for permission to inspect; define recurring trigger and service level; report revenue, cost, acceptance, correction, and use separately.

**Economics:** Recurring contribution = collected recurring fee minus model/tool cost, operator/support time, acquisition allocation, refunds, and incident cost.

**Practice:** Publish a truthful proof pack (50 practice minutes): sample/checklist, claim ledger, case-study skeleton, three permission-based outreach drafts, and retainer scorecard. No outreach is sent. Every claim exposes whether it is measured, self-reported, inferred, or unknown.

**Quality boundary:** never fabricate testimonials, identity, results, affiliations, or typical outcomes; never use scraped data, fake accounts, evasion, unsolicited automated replies, or unlicensed social media.

**Real UI:** none.<br>
**Source IDs:** `aup`, `x-automation-policy`, `github-seo-audit`, `github-lastsaas`, `x-codyschneider`, `x-wcandillon`, `docs-cost`.

### Lesson 12: Capstone: run a seven-day demand test — 120 minutes

**Objective:** Complete a buyer-evidence sprint with an offer, sample, verification trail, economics, outreach review, and decision rubric.

**Core instruction:** Before Day 1, freeze the low-risk buyer, trigger, sample, behavioral threshold, budget, privacy boundary, and stop rule. Do not set an income target. The sequence collects recent-problem evidence, creates a scoped sample/checks, tests and corrects the workflow, requests permission-based review, records behavior/cost, and audits evidence, quality, economics, policy, privacy, and rights. Go requires strong problem evidence, accepted sample, viable cost range, compliant route, and concrete paid next step. Revise names one bounded second test. Stop preserves evidence when pain, access, safety, acceptance, or economics fail.

**Seven-day sequence:**

1. Day 1: freeze thesis, thresholds, budget, sources, data boundary, and stop rule.
2. Day 2: conduct at least five recent-problem interviews or document failed recruitment.
3. Day 3: create the two-page scope, delivery spec, and bounded sample.
4. Day 4: test normal and adversarial fixtures, correct the sample, and log changes.
5. Day 5: request permission-based review from at least five fit-qualified people.
6. Day 6: record behavior, cost, quality, corrections, and concrete next steps.
7. Day 7: audit sources, rights, privacy, policy, and economics; decide go, revise, or stop.

**Portfolio:** money thesis/stop rule; de-identified evidence matrix and denominator; scope/cost scenarios; delivery spec/fixtures; sample/acceptance record; outreach log; source/claim ledger; rights/privacy/policy/economics audit; final decision memo.

**Economics:** Capstone decision = buyer evidence + accepted delivery + viable contribution range + compliant operating path. One missing critical condition can justify revise or stop.

**Quality boundary:** preserve silence, declines, corrections, deviations, unresolved risks, practitioner caveats, source dates, interface dates, and the no-income-guarantee notice. Use only consented interviews and synthetic or approved data.

**Real UI:** `fig-03-tools-menu`, `fig-06-artifact-workspace`.<br>
**Source IDs:** `academy-work`, `docs-prompting`, `help-research`, `docs-cost`, `commercial-terms`, `aup`, `x-automation-policy`.
