# Research brief: How to make money with Codex

Research cut-off: 2026-08-23<br>
Official OpenAI docs revalidated: 2026-08-24<br>
Course: aicourse.top Course 11<br>
Primary question: What can the available evidence responsibly teach about using Codex in work that may lead to paid revenue or economic value?

## Executive finding

Codex can increase a provider's capacity to investigate, specify, build, test, review, document, and hand off technical work. It cannot create customer demand, trust, distribution, payment, acceptance, or profit by itself. The defensible course is therefore an evidence-led commercialisation workflow:

1. choose a reachable payer and a costly job;
2. validate the job before building;
3. write a bounded commercial specification;
4. work in an authorised environment;
5. build and verify a small pilot;
6. price from full delivery economics;
7. sell with inspectable proof;
8. deliver with review and acceptance controls;
9. reuse only stable, non-secret process knowledge; and
10. automate recurring work only after a manual workflow is proven.

The strongest direct commercial signal located is an OpenAI Academy case about Goliath Data. The case reports that a prospect requested a $10,000-per-month contract after seeing a tailored feature and that Goliath estimated about $40 of compute. It does not report that the contract was signed, that money was collected, that the work was profitable, or that Codex alone caused the outcome. The course must preserve all four limits whenever it uses the case.

No reviewed source establishes typical earnings from Codex. No reviewed source justifies an earnings guarantee, a passive-income promise, or a standard markup from compute cost to client price.

## Method and evidence rules

The review prioritised first-party OpenAI Academy cases and official product documentation for capability and workflow claims. Public GitHub repositories and discussions were used to establish inspectable artefacts, public offers, and delivery practices. X posts were treated as dated practitioner anecdotes and figure locators, not audited business evidence.

Every source was assigned one evidence class:

| Evidence class | What it may establish | What it may not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official workflow | A documented feature, control, or recommended workflow | Demand, price, sales, revenue, or profit |
| Reported commercial signal | A named source reports a contract request, growth, or another commercial event | Audited profit, typical return, or single-cause attribution |
| Paid offer | A creator publicly offers a paid tier or reports productisation intent | Customers, collected revenue, margin, or profit |
| Business process | A demo, product, time saving, reach figure, review, or handoff exists | A paying market or financial result |
| Scope exclusion | A tempting source is outside the Codex outcome boundary | Any Codex-caused commercial result |

The audit keeps these measures distinct:

- an artefact existing;
- a demo being shown;
- users, downloads, stars, followers, or reach;
- self-reported time saved or faster builds;
- an offer or checkout existing;
- a request for a contract;
- a signed contract;
- collected revenue;
- contribution margin; and
- profit after all costs.

Moving from one level to another requires new evidence. The course does not infer later levels from earlier ones.

## Key findings and safe use

### 1. Goliath Data is a commercial signal, not a return-on-investment formula

[OpenAI Academy's Goliath Data case](https://academy.openai.com/public/blogs/goliath-data-from-three-people-to-20-in-chattanooga) reports a prospect's request for a $10,000-per-month contract after a tailored feature demo, an estimated compute cost of about $40, and broader company-reported growth and operational results.

Safe use: demonstrate why a specific feature tied to a buyer's workflow can create a strong commercial conversation.

Required limits: the article does not confirm signature, collection, profit, typicality, or Codex-only causation. The compute estimate is not total delivery cost. It excludes at least discovery, labour, acquisition, review, infrastructure, support, revisions, tax, risk, and overhead.

### 2. Proaction supports a customer-specific pilot workflow

[OpenAI Academy's Proaction case](https://academy.openai.com/public/blogs/colin-knudsen-proaction-codex-customer-demos) describes a five-person startup using a sandboxed, customer-specific prototype and handing engineers transcripts, screenshots, and a working artefact. The account says the company moved to the front of an 18-vendor evaluation.

Safe use: teach the path from customer conversation to a bounded, non-production demo and evidence-rich engineering handoff.

Required limits: the case reports no sale, price, revenue, profit, or reproducible conversion rate.

### 3. Marco Salsiccia supports shipping and accessibility workflow claims

[OpenAI Academy's Marco Salsiccia case](https://academy.openai.com/public/blogs/marco-salsiccia-builds-accessible-apps-with-codex) reports five iOS apps, two Android apps, several web apps within eight months, more than 11,500 users for Whack A Braille, a web-to-iOS conversion in less than a day, and getting WordBopper running on the APH Monarch in about four minutes while learning the SDK. It also retains human accessibility testing as part of the process.

Safe use: show that Codex can contribute to rapid cross-platform delivery while human domain testing remains essential.

Required limits: applications, users, and speed do not establish revenue or profit. Optional in-app purchases on a store listing would establish an offer, not purchases.

### 4. TokenGauge supports a live-offer and inspectable-artefact claim

The [TokenGauge discussion in openai/codex](https://github.com/openai/codex/discussions/38815) describes using Codex for research, typed data, calculators, tests, browser verification, and a product with optional one-time paid tiers. The [public repository](https://github.com/fablgen-agent/tokengauge) makes the artefact inspectable.

Safe use: demonstrate productisation steps, public source, tests, and an explicit paid offer.

Required limits: GitHub hosting does not imply OpenAI endorsement. Neither the discussion nor repository confirms customers, collected revenue, cost, or profit.

### 5. Productisation intent on X is not sales evidence

In the [ashebytes post](https://x.com/ashebytes/status/2029374748061516164), the creator says that interest led them to productise a Codex-built video hub.

Safe use: show a transition from an artefact to a packaged offer.

Required limits: the post reports no price, buyers, collected revenue, cost, or profit.

### 6. Practitioner posts are useful for workflow and output examples

The reviewed posts include a [Tuist clean-build migration](https://x.com/pepicrft/status/2019079104029442206), [Codex desktop and MVP work](https://x.com/EstePrimeWorld/status/2081217288414409071), a [rapid Three.js demo](https://x.com/fkadev/status/2082567881171050496), a [3D-printer dashboard](https://x.com/billyjhowell/status/2082824949195022403), an [experimental EPUB reader](https://x.com/devze_ro/status/2082886427600457833), [manually checked review findings](https://x.com/nateberkopec/status/2015977728621871370), a [Codex desktop workspace](https://x.com/flavioAd/status/2018423646314807569), a [CLI plan-mode account](https://x.com/kr0der/status/2015711721092948064), a [CLI fix summary](https://x.com/rudrank/status/2015694268929909129), and a [pull-request handoff](https://x.com/steipete/status/2010895856338088212).

Safe use: illustrate dated interfaces, practitioner-reported process, artefacts, verification, and handoff patterns.

Required limits: these are self-reports. Build time is not profit; a product image is not production verification; a review anecdote is not an accuracy rate; a public handoff is not proof of safety in another repository.

### 7. The contractor case is an explicit exclusion

[How ChatGPT Built a Contractor's Back Office](https://academy.openai.com/public/blogs/how-chatgpt-became-a-contractors-back-office-2026-05-07) reports outcomes from ChatGPT workflows and describes Codex as a future plan. It must not be cited as evidence that Codex caused the reported time, inbound, or revenue outcomes.

## Course architecture derived from the evidence

| Lesson | Decision or artefact | Primary evidence role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money, not magic | Claim ledger and falsifiable commercial experiment | Separates capability from commercial outcome |
| Choose a market wedge | Reachable payer, costly job, narrow outcome | Proaction, Marco, TokenGauge |
| Validate before building | Interview, sample input, smoke test, stop condition | Customer-specific pilot pattern |
| Write the commercial specification | Outcome, scope, constraints, acceptance tests | Official prompting and bootcamp workflow |
| Protect client work | Authorisation, environment, least privilege, data boundary | Security, authentication, worktrees |
| Build a verified pilot | Small artefact plus test and review evidence | Official review guidance and community output examples |
| Price for margin | Full-cost floor and labelled scenarios | Goliath boundary plus live account costs |
| Sell with proof | Narrow case evidence without fabricated metrics | Proaction, Goliath, Marco, TokenGauge, X |
| Deliver with control | Separated changes, review, acceptance, rollback | Code review, worktrees, Action, public handoffs |
| Productise reuse | Reusable instructions and skills without secrets | AGENTS.md, skills, inspectable product example |
| Retain and automate | Proven recurring workflow, monitoring, escalation, and a course-authored operational-value worksheet that keeps measured inputs separate | Automations, Action, business operations support the workflow; they do not establish a financial formula |
| Launch capstone | A reviewable go/no-go commercialisation experiment | Entire evidence chain |

## Publication and instructional controls

The release should enforce these corrections:

- Never describe Codex as a passive-income system.
- Never convert a request for a contract into signed or collected revenue.
- Never convert compute cost into total cost or profit.
- Never convert stars, users, downloads, reach, or a checkout into customers or revenue.
- Never convert self-reported time saved into cash savings or profit without a measured causal model.
- Never present a demo as production-ready.
- Never imply that an automated scanner provides compliance, security certification, or a warranty.
- Never let Codex send outreach, accept terms, charge a customer, merge, deploy, or access client systems without specific authority and a human decision gate.
- Never hard-code a current product price as durable course truth. Label calculator inputs as learner-entered or fictional examples, with an observation date.
- Label OpenAI Academy company cases as reported cases, not expected learner outcomes.
- Label GitHub and X material as public artefact or anecdotal process evidence, not OpenAI endorsement.
- Treat non-English Course 11 routes as localized navigation to the audited English edition, not complete translations; independent native-speaker review remains pending.

## Figure standard

The course contains nine evidence figures. Four carry authentic Codex interface or transcript evidence: two current first-party Codex desktop crops from a disposable workspace, one official historical CLI illustration, and one first-party rendering of an actual read-only Codex CLI transcript. The other five are first-party synthetic teaching fixtures: three product outputs and two repository handoffs. Captions state these classifications explicitly. Product UI is dated and can change, so current official documentation governs when it differs. Provenance, digests, visible identifiers, privacy review, transformations, and evidence limits are recorded in `figure-provenance.md`, the first-party rights record, and the public figure notice.

The official historical CLI figure comes from the Apache-2.0-licensed `openai/codex` repository at an immutable commit. The other eight use first-party pixels and invented data, with no creator-post image pixels redistributed. Their X links remain practitioner context and their inclusion on an OpenAI community page remains a provenance locator, not independent verification or endorsement. This resolves the earlier reproduction-rights gap while preserving the distinction between authentic Codex UI evidence, contextual practitioner claims, and synthetic downstream artefacts.

## Remaining evidence gaps

The reviewed corpus does not provide:

- audited Codex-attributable sales, revenue, cost, or profit distributions;
- a reliable base rate for converting Codex demos into contracts;
- independent measurement of the practitioner timing claims;
- a causal estimate that separates Codex from operator expertise, customer access, product quality, brand, distribution, or other tools;
- typical support, acquisition, revision, insurance, tax, and failure costs for Codex-assisted services; or
- a universal legal, security, privacy, accessibility, or professional-compliance determination.

Those gaps are not defects to hide. They define the learner's job: run a bounded experiment, collect first-party evidence, update the economics, and stop when the evidence does not support the offer.
