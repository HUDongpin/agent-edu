# Course 12 research brief

## How to Make Money with Claude

**Course ID:** `claude-income`<br>
**Public sequence:** Course 12, assigned literally rather than inferred from the current catalogue<br>
**Research cut-off:** 2026-08-23; live-source recheck 2026-08-24<br>
**Content language:** English<br>
**Release posture:** Evidence-bounded, no guaranteed income, current facts date-stamped

## Executive conclusion

Claude can support commercially useful research, writing, analysis, file
production, workflow execution, prototyping, software development, and API
products. Those capabilities do not prove buyer demand or business success.
The defensible course is therefore a business-experiment course with Claude as
leverage, not a list of income claims or automation tricks.

The instructional spine is:

1. choose a problem within the learner's verification expertise;
2. test recent buyer pain and willingness to take a concrete next step;
3. scope and price a bounded deliverable, including human review and risk;
4. deliver through isolated context, source checks, editable files, and
   acceptance tests;
5. standardize only after repeated corrections reveal a stable workflow;
6. prototype and write software only after user behavior supports investment;
7. sell with attributed, narrow proof and compliant, permission-based contact;
8. finish with a go, revise, or stop decision rather than an earnings claim.

The course has four units, 12 lessons, 12 hands-on deliverables, 24 scenario
questions, a balanced 16-question final assessment, and a 100-point capstone.
Seven first-party captures show the real Claude Desktop interface as observed
on the research date.

## Non-negotiable outcome disclaimer

> This course teaches practical ways to use Claude in services, workflows,
> software, and product experiments. It does not promise income or business
> success. Results depend on your expertise, market demand, sales, execution,
> quality control, costs, and compliance with applicable terms and law. Verify
> Claude's outputs before using or selling them.

Practitioner examples are attributed self-reports and are not representative
outcome evidence. Revenue screenshots, projections, views, likes, and finished
artifacts do not establish profit, causality, typicality, or repeatable demand.

## Research questions

1. Which Claude capabilities have direct current official support?
2. Which commercially useful workflows can be inferred without claiming
   earnings?
3. What do inspectible GitHub repositories reveal about repeatable delivery?
4. What do practitioner posts reveal, and what can they not establish?
5. Which current plan, interface, legal, policy, and cost facts are volatile?
6. Which learning and release gates prevent the course from becoming hype?

## Official findings and course corrections

### Claude for Work and Academy

[Claude for Work](https://academy.claude.com/collections/claude-for-work)
and the [Academy tutorial catalogue](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials)
support workplace applications and current learning workflows. They establish
capability examples, not willingness to pay. The course adds customer discovery,
paid-pilot scoping, verification, and economics before any product claim.

### Projects

The current [Projects help page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9517075-what-are-projects)
states that Projects are available on all plans. As reviewed on 2026-08-23,
Free users can create up to five Projects and enhanced retrieval is a paid-plan
feature. This corrects older or oversimplified claims that Projects are paid-only,
unavailable on Free, or unlimited. The lesson treats this as a volatile dated
fact and teaches one client and one outcome per Project.

### Artifacts

The current [Artifacts help page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9487310-what-are-artifacts-and-how-do-i-use-them)
supports documents, code, web pages, SVG graphics, diagrams, React components,
and some AI-powered apps. Iteration, versioning, download, publishing, and
integration behavior varies by current product context. As rechecked on
2026-08-24, Code execution and file creation must be enabled for Artifacts to
work. Publishing is not
checkout, payment, customer acquisition, support, privacy, or production
readiness. Legacy Remix language is excluded.

### Skills

The current [Skills help page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512180-use-skills-in-claude)
and current Academy material indicate availability across Free, Pro, Max, Team,
and Enterprise. Use requires Code execution and file creation to be enabled;
Team and Enterprise access can also depend on organization and administrator
settings. This takes
precedence over narrower paid-plan wording observed in older or non-controlling
material. The course date-stamps the claim and teaches version, author, fixture,
license, and rollback review.

### Connectors

The [Connectors help page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11176164-use-connectors-to-extend-claude-s-capabilities)
supports access to connected services under the account's permissions. The
course treats those permissions as a data and action boundary. It starts with
read-only scope, synthetic fixtures, dedicated accounts where possible, and
human approval for external effects.

### Cowork safety

The current [Cowork safety guidance](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13364135-use-claude-cowork-safely)
states that Cowork sessions run in Anthropic's cloud and local files accessed by
Cowork are processed there. Approval behavior varies by mode and tool. Skip all
approvals does not check actions, and computer use lacks ordinary per-action
tool checks, although permanent deletion still requires explicit permission.
The course therefore teaches a dedicated folder, approved copies, synthetic
testing, and human review for consequential actions.

### Research and Web search

The [Research help page](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11088861-use-research-on-claude)
describes iterative citation-bearing investigation for eligible paid plans and
its dependency on Web search. The observed 2026-08-23 Claude Desktop interface
places Research and Web search in the composer plus menu. The course labels this
as dated interface evidence and teaches claim-to-source verification after
Research returns citations.

### Claude Code

[Claude Code 101](https://academy.claude.com/courses/claude-code-101)
supports the software workflow foundation. Anthropic's
[Auto mode announcement](https://claude.com/blog/auto-mode-default-in-claude-code)
states that Auto became the default for new Pro, Max, and Team sessions from
2026-08-14, subject to configuration. The course does not turn a default into
authorization. It requires repository boundaries, tests, human review, secrets
control, rollback, and incident ownership.

### Prompting and API economics

Anthropic's [prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
support explicit instructions, context, examples, structure, and output formats.
The course turns those principles into a delivery specification with missing
evidence behavior and independent acceptance.

The [cost optimization documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
supports model selection, prompt caching, token discipline, eligible batch
processing, and spend controls. It does not prove lower total business cost.
The course uses a dated current-price input and measures model, tool, retry,
retrieval, observability, review, support, and escalation cost per accepted
outcome. No exact model price is embedded in the curriculum.

### Commercial and policy boundary

Anthropic's [Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
allocate rights between Anthropic and eligible commercial customers, to the
extent permitted by law. They do not certify output accuracy, originality,
copyrightability, non-infringement, or fitness for a client decision. The
operator independently verifies the output and handles client agreement,
third-party rights, and applicable law.

The [Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup) prohibits deceptive,
fraudulent, privacy-invasive, and other harmful uses and sets safeguards for
higher-risk activity. Course exercises exclude live billing, production
deployment, unsolicited automation, professional advice outside qualification,
and high-impact decisions without appropriate safeguards.

## Instructional architecture

### Unit 1: Prove value before you build

- Choose a service, managed workflow, or software path based on verification
  expertise and time to evidence.
- Validate recent buyer behavior instead of hypothetical interest.
- Write an acceptance-tested scope and full-cost price floor.

### Unit 2: Deliver work a client can inspect

- Convert scope into a stable delivery specification and separate case inputs.
- Isolate each client outcome in a Project and least-privilege folder.
- Deliver citation-grade research with claim class, source fit, and uncertainty.

### Unit 3: Turn delivery into an operating system

- Produce editable files and inspect the actual target format.
- Version and regression-test Skills; map Connector permissions.
- Use Artifacts as evidence-buying prototypes, not payment or demand proof.

### Unit 4: Build, sell, and retain responsibly

- Build one validated software slice with Code and measured API unit economics.
- Publish only narrow, permissioned proof and use compliant outreach.
- Complete a seven-day demand test with a go, revise, or stop decision.

## Assessment contract

The formative bank has 24 scenario questions, two per lesson. The final quiz
selects 16 questions, four from each unit, with at least one critical-boundary
item per unit. Passing requires at least 13 correct and every selected critical
item correct. The bank avoids current prices, model labels, and permanent screen
positions.

The capstone rubric totals 100 points:

- buyer evidence, 15;
- scope and economics, 15;
- workflow quality, 15;
- deliverable usefulness, 15;
- independent verification, 20;
- privacy, rights, safety, and compliance, 10;
- experiment and decision, 10.

Passing requires at least 80 points and no critical failure. Fabricated evidence,
exposed secrets or personal data, spam or deception, unsupported income claims,
unreviewed production or billing, missing critical verification, unlicensed
reuse, or concealed failures prevents completion until corrected.

## Release standard

Release is permitted only when the offline release checker, source-link audit,
media-rights and privacy audit, TypeScript, lint for Course 12 files, static
build, and Playwright acceptance suite pass. The published build must be checked
at 390, 768, and 1440 pixels, in light and dark themes, with keyboard-only
navigation, reduced motion, no JavaScript for core content, denied storage, and
an English-only content disclosure on non-English site shells.

The release audit must record corrections against the independent HOLD report.
The source report remains unchanged as an audit trail; closure is documented in
the separate release checklist.
